Hygiene ও Infection-Control Process

Wet cupping-এ blood exposure হয়। তাই শুধু “sterile equipment” বলা যথেষ্ট নয়—প্রতিটি ধাপ নিরাপদভাবে অনুসরণ করা প্রয়োজন।

  1. Hand hygiene ও clean surface: Session-এর আগে হাত ও কাজের জায়গা প্রস্তুত করা।
  2. New gloves ও skin assessment: Skin-এর অবস্থা পরীক্ষা করে antiseptic preparation করা।
  3. Single-use skin-piercing item: Lancet বা blade একবার ব্যবহার করে sharps container-এ ফেলা।
  4. Appropriate cup handling: Cup-এর ধরন অনুযায়ী disposable অথবা সঠিকভাবে processed equipment ব্যবহার করা।
  5. Dressing ও waste disposal: প্রয়োজনীয় dressing, contaminated waste ও sharps নিরাপদভাবে সরানো।
  6. Aftercare: Skin care, expected marks ও warning signs বুঝিয়ে দেওয়া।

6. What is Hijama therapy?

Hijama is a form of cupping in which suction is applied to the skin. Wet cupping also involves controlled superficial skin punctures, while dry cupping does not pierce the skin.

7. What is the difference between wet and dry cupping?

Dry cupping uses suction without piercing the skin. Wet cupping combines suction with controlled superficial skin punctures and requires additional infection-control and safe sharps-disposal procedures.

8. Is Hijama scientifically proven?

Research suggests cupping may provide short-term relief for certain pain conditions in some people. Much of the evidence is low or moderate quality, and it does not establish that Hijama cures diseases.

9. Can Hijama cure back pain?

Hijama should not be described as a cure for back pain. Some people may experience short-term relief, but persistent, severe or radiating back pain requires clinical assessment.

10. Can Hijama help with neck and shoulder discomfort?

Some research suggests cupping may reduce neck-related discomfort for certain people, but the evidence has limitations. It should be considered complementary care rather than a replacement for medical treatment or rehabilitation.

11. Can Hijama treat migraine?

Evidence regarding Hijama for migraine remains uncertain. Severe, sudden, recurrent or changing headaches require proper medical assessment.

12. Does Hijama remove toxins from the body?

There is insufficient high-quality evidence that Hijama removes unspecified toxins from the body. The kidneys, liver, lungs and digestive system normally handle waste-processing functions.

13. Does Hijama boost immunity?

There is not enough reliable clinical evidence to claim that Hijama boosts immunity or prevents diseases. It should not replace vaccination, medical advice or prescribed treatment.

14. Is Hijama safe?

Hijama can cause side effects and is not risk-free. Screening, skin preparation, single-use skin-piercing equipment, infection-control procedures and aftercare help reduce avoidable risks.

15. What are the possible side effects of Hijama?

Possible effects include temporary marks, soreness, skin irritation, dizziness, bleeding, infection, scarring or persistent discolouration. Repeated wet cupping may contribute to anaemia in susceptible individuals.

16. Does Hijama hurt?

Dry cupping may produce pressure or pulling on the skin. Wet cupping can cause brief discomfort during superficial skin puncturing, and pain tolerance varies.

17. How long do Hijama marks last?

Cupping commonly leaves temporary circular marks. Their colour and duration vary with skin response, suction strength and individual factors.

18. Who should speak with a doctor before wet cupping?

People with bleeding disorders, severe anaemia, poor wound healing, significant chronic illness, pregnancy, active skin disease or blood-thinning medication should obtain appropriate medical advice before wet cupping.

19. Can someone taking blood thinners receive Hijama?

Wet cupping may increase bleeding risk for people taking anticoagulant or antiplatelet medication. Do not stop prescribed medicine yourself; discuss suitability with the prescribing clinician and practitioner.

20. Is Hijama suitable during pregnancy?

Safety evidence during pregnancy is limited. A pregnant person should consult their obstetric healthcare professional before considering cupping.

21. Can a person with diabetes receive Hijama?

Diabetes can affect skin healing and infection risk, particularly when poorly controlled. The practitioner must be informed, and medical advice may be necessary before wet cupping.

22. Can Hijama be performed over infected or damaged skin?

Cupping should not be applied over active infection, open wounds, inflamed skin or unexplained lesions. Medical assessment may be required.

23. What screening is required before Hijama?

Screening should cover symptoms, medical conditions, medication, bleeding history, anaemia, allergies, pregnancy status, previous reactions and the condition of the intended treatment area.

24. How should I prepare for a Hijama appointment?

Provide accurate medical and medication information, keep the treatment area clean and wear comfortable clothing. Do not stop medication unless advised by an appropriate healthcare professional.

25. What happens during a home Hijama session?

The practitioner reviews health information, examines the intended skin area, explains the procedure and obtains consent. The selected procedure is followed by skin care and aftercare guidance.

26. How long does a Hijama session take?

A session commonly takes approximately 30–60 minutes, although time varies with screening, type of cupping, number of sites and individual needs.

27. What equipment is brought for a home session?

Supplies may include cups, suction equipment, gloves, antiseptic, single-use skin-piercing equipment, dressings, waste materials and a sharps container. The provider should explain which items are disposable.

28. How should I care for the skin after wet cupping?

Follow the dressing and skin-care instructions, keep the area appropriately clean and monitor for worsening pain, swelling, discharge, persistent bleeding or fever. Seek medical attention for concerning symptoms.
