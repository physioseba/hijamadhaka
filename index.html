Safety First

Before You Choose Wet Cupping

Wet cupping involves small superficial skin openings and blood exposure, so medical history, infection-control precautions, realistic expectations, and aftercare matter.

Tell the practitioner before wet cupping if you have:

  • Blood-thinner or anticoagulant medication use
  • Known bleeding disorder or significant anemia
  • Pregnancy or recent delivery
  • Active skin infection, open wound, or inflamed skin
  • Diabetes with poor healing, neuropathy, or recurrent skin problems

Possible side effects

Cupping can leave temporary circular marks and may cause soreness or skin irritation. Reported adverse effects include persistent discoloration, scars, burns, and infection. Repeated wet cupping has also been associated with rare blood-loss anemia.

When medical care comes first

Chest pain, severe shortness of breath, sudden weakness or speech difficulty, uncontrolled bleeding, fainting, major trauma, or rapidly worsening infection symptoms require urgent medical assessment rather than a cupping appointment.

Evidence & expectations

Some research reports short-term benefit for certain pain conditions such as low-back pain, but results are not a guarantee for every person or condition. Hijama should not replace appropriate diagnosis or evidence-based treatment when those are needed.

Important: Do not stop prescribed blood-thinning or other medication for Hijama unless the prescribing clinician tells you to do so.
