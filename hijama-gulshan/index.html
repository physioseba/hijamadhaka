What Is Hijama / Cupping Therapy?

Hijama is a traditional therapy in which cups are placed on the skin to create suction, drawing blood to the surface. It is traditionally used to support circulation and relaxation, and is practiced in both a dry form (suction only) and a wet form (suction combined with small, controlled incisions).

Wet Cupping vs. Dry Cupping

Wet cupping combines suction with light, controlled incisions to draw a small amount of blood to the surface. Dry cupping uses suction alone, without any incision, and is often chosen by first-time clients or for muscular tension.

Hijama as Sunnah (Prophetic Medicine)

Hijama has a long history within Islamic tradition and is commonly associated with the practice of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him). Before & After Your Hijama Session

Pre-Session Preparation

  • Eat a light meal 2–3 hours before your session — avoid arriving hungry or immediately after a heavy meal.
  • Stay well hydrated in the 24 hours before your appointment.
  • Wear loose, comfortable clothing that allows easy access to the treatment area.

Aftercare Instructions

  • Keep the treated area clean and dry for 24 hours; avoid direct sun exposure and swimming.
  • Drink plenty of water and rest for the remainder of the day.
  • Mild bruising or marks are common and typically fade within 3–10 days.

Who Should Avoid Hijama (Contraindications)

  • Pregnant women
  • People taking blood-thinning medication or with bleeding disorders
  • Active skin infections or open wounds at the treatment site
  • Uncontrolled chronic conditions — please consult your physician first

Frequently Asked Questions

What is hijama?

Hijama is a traditional cupping therapy where cups are placed on the skin to create suction, drawing blood to the surface. It is performed as wet cupping (with small incisions) or dry cupping (suction only).

Is hijama painful?

Most clients describe a pulling or tight sensation rather than sharp pain. Any discomfort during wet cupping is typically brief, and pressure is adjusted to individual tolerance.

Who should avoid hijama therapy?

Hijama is generally not recommended for pregnant women, people on blood-thinning medication, or anyone with active skin infections at the treatment site. Consult a physician if you have a chronic condition.
