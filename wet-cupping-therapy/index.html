Understand the Treatment

What Is Wet Cupping Therapy?

Wet cupping — the classical form of hijama — is a Sunnah practice traced to the Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) and used across traditional Islamic, Chinese, and Greek medicine. A cup is first placed on the skin to create suction and mark the area; it's removed, small superficial nicks are made, and a second, brief suction draws out a small amount of blood.

Traditionally this is described as removing "stagnant blood" and supporting the body's circulation. It's worth being clear-eyed: while many people find the session relaxing and report feeling better afterward, robust clinical evidence for specific medical claims is still limited, so we treat wet cupping as a complementary wellness practice — not a replacement for medical diagnosis or treatment of any condition.

Wet Cupping vs. Dry Cupping

Wet cupping includes the small incision step and is the classical hijama method most requested by clients. Dry cupping uses suction only, with no skin break — a good option if you're new to cupping or prefer to avoid incisions. Learn more about dry cupping.

What the Session Feels Like

Most clients describe the initial suction as mild pressure, with a brief pinching sensation during the small incisions. Sessions typically run 30–60 minutes, and circular marks from the cups usually fade within days.

Traditional & Reported Benefits

Why People Try Wet Cupping

These are the reasons clients most often ask for wet cupping. We describe them as traditionally believed or commonly reported benefits, not guaranteed medical outcomes.

Muscle & Joint Tension

Commonly used for back, neck, and shoulder tightness, and general muscular stiffness.

Circulation Support

The suction is believed to temporarily boost local blood flow to the treated area.

Relaxation & Stress Relief

Many clients find the session itself calming, and use it as part of a broader stress-management routine.

Post-Activity Recovery

Some athletes and active clients use cupping alongside rest and stretching after exertion.

General Wellness

Regular sessions are often chosen as a preventive, whole-body wellness habit rather than for one specific complaint.

Sunnah Practice

For many clients in Dhaka, the spiritual and traditional significance of hijama is itself a key reason to choose it.

If you're seeking cupping for a specific diagnosed condition (chronic pain, a blood pressure concern, a skin condition, etc.), please discuss it with your doctor first — cupping can be a helpful complement to medical care, but it isn't a substitute for it.

Please Read

Is Wet Cupping Right for You? Safety & Who Should Avoid It

⚠️ Speak to a doctor first if any of the following apply to you:

  • Pregnancy
  • Bleeding disorders or severe anemia
  • Currently taking blood thinners
  • Active skin infection, open wounds, or a flare-up of a skin condition at the treatment site
  • Uncontrolled chronic illness (e.g., unmanaged diabetes or heart conditions)

If none of these apply, wet cupping performed by a certified practitioner with sterile, disposable equipment is generally well tolerated. Mild bruising or circular marks that fade within a few days are a normal, expected part of the process.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is wet cupping (hijama)?

It's the classical hijama method: cups create suction to mark the area, small superficial skin nicks are made, and a second suction draws out a small amount of blood.

Is wet cupping painful?

Most clients describe mild suction pressure and a brief pinching sensation during the small incisions. It's generally well tolerated.

What's the difference between wet and dry cupping?

Wet cupping includes small skin incisions; dry cupping uses suction only, with no skin break.

How long does a session take?

Typically 30–60 minutes, depending on the number of points treated.

Do you provide home service in Dhaka?

Is the equipment sterile?

Yes — sterile cups, disposable blades, gloves, and antiseptic materials are used at every session, with no exceptions.

Is a female therapist available?

Yes, female practitioners are available for home visits on request.

How much does wet cupping cost in Dhaka?

Pricing depends on the number of cupping points and location; contact us on WhatsApp or call for the current rate.

How often should I get wet cupping?

Many clients space sessions a few weeks to a few months apart; your practitioner can suggest a schedule based on your goals.

Who should avoid wet cupping?

Pregnant women, people with bleeding disorders, severe anemia, or those on blood thinners should consult a doctor first.

Will wet cupping leave marks?

Mild bruising or circular marks are common and usually fade within a few days to a week.

Is wet cupping a Sunnah practice?

Yes, hijama has roots in Sunnah tradition, which is part of why many clients in Dhaka choose it alongside its physical benefits.

Can wet cupping cure my medical condition?

No — wet cupping is a complementary wellness practice, not a proven cure for any medical condition. Please see a doctor for diagnosis and treatment of specific health issues.

What should I do before my session?

Avoid a heavy meal right before, stay hydrated, and tell your practitioner about any medical conditions or medications.

What should I do after my session?

Keep the treated area clean and dry for the rest of the day, avoid direct sun exposure on cupped skin, and follow your practitioner's aftercare notes.
