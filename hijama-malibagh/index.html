Understanding the Therapy

What Is Hijama (Cupping) Therapy?

Hijama uses specialized cups to create gentle suction on the skin. In wet cupping — the traditional form of hijama — small, controlled incisions are made after suction to allow a small amount of blood to be drawn out. It has roots in traditional and Islamic medicine and remains widely practiced across Bangladesh today.

Wet Cupping vs. Dry Cupping

Dry cupping uses suction only, with no incisions — often used for muscle tension. Wet cupping (true hijama) adds small, controlled incisions after suction. Our Malibagh therapists offer both: see Wet Cupping Therapy and Dry Cupping Therapy for full details.

Is Hijama Sunnah?

Hijama has a clear basis in Islamic tradition, with certain days often recommended for the practice. We perform every session respectfully within this tradition, alongside sound hygiene and safety standards.

Therapy Basics

What is hijama therapy?

Hijama is a traditional cupping therapy where cups create suction on the skin; in wet cupping, small controlled incisions allow a small amount of blood to be drawn out.

What is the difference between wet cupping and dry cupping?

Dry cupping uses suction only. Wet cupping (true hijama) adds small, controlled incisions after suction.

Is hijama Sunnah?

Yes, hijama has a basis in Islamic tradition and is often performed on specific recommended days.

Is hijama therapy different from acupuncture?

Yes. Acupuncture uses thin needles at specific points, while hijama uses suction cups and, in wet cupping, controlled incisions.

Is hijama painful?

Most clients report mild discomfort — a brief pinching sensation — followed by a feeling of lightness afterward.

Safety & Health

Is hijama safe?

When performed by a trained therapist using sterilized, single-use equipment, hijama is generally considered safe.

What are the side effects of hijama?

The most common effects are mild bruising or circular marks, which typically fade within one to two weeks.

Who should avoid hijama?

People with bleeding disorders, those on blood-thinning medication, pregnant women (site-dependent), and those with active skin infections should consult a doctor first.

Can hijama cure diabetes or high blood pressure?

No. Hijama may offer complementary support for wellness, but it is not a cure or replacement for medical treatment.

Do you use sterilized or single-use equipment?

Yes, single-use or properly sterilized cupping sets and disposable blades/needles are used for every client.

Can hijama leave permanent marks?

No, the circular marks are typically temporary and fade within one to two weeks in most cases.

What conditions can hijama help with?

Back pain, neck and shoulder stiffness, migraines, joint pain, and general fatigue — as a complementary therapy.

Can hijama help with migraines and headaches?

Many clients seek hijama for migraine relief; results vary, and it should complement, not replace, medical care.

Does hijama help with stress and anxiety?

Some clients report feeling more relaxed after a session, similar to a massage, as a subjective complementary benefit.
