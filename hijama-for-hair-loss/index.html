Understand the Treatment

What Is Hijama for Hair Loss?

Hijama is traditional cupping therapy applied to the scalp, crown, and nape of the neck. In wet cupping, cups create suction to mark the treatment area, then a therapist makes very small, superficial skin nicks before a second, brief suction draws out a small amount of blood. In dry cupping, only suction is used — no skin is broken, which suits clients who prefer a needle-free option.

The traditional belief is that this local suction supports blood circulation to the scalp and may help deliver nutrients to hair follicles. It's important to be upfront: there isn't strong clinical-trial evidence proving hijama regrows hair, so we present it as a complementary practice — not a replacement for a proper diagnosis of what's actually causing your hair loss.

How Cupping May Support Scalp Circulation

The suction created by the cup is thought to temporarily increase local blood flow, which is why cupping is traditionally applied over areas of tension or reduced circulation. Some practitioners believe this may help reduce oxidative stress on the scalp. These are traditional explanations rather than settled clinical findings, and results differ from person to person.

Wet Cupping vs. Dry Cupping for Hair Fall

Wet cupping follows the classical hijama method with small skin nicks and is the most requested option. Dry/machine cupping skips the incision step entirely and is a good starting point if you're new to hijama or simply prefer not to shave the treatment area. Your practitioner will help you choose during the consultation.

Know Before You Book

Common Causes of Hair Loss We Get Asked About

We don't diagnose hair loss — that's a dermatologist's job — but understanding the likely cause helps you and your practitioner set realistic expectations for what cupping can support.

Androgenetic Alopecia (Pattern Baldness)

The most common cause of hair thinning, largely genetic and hormone-driven (linked to DHT). In men it often shows as a receding hairline or crown thinning; in women it's usually more diffuse. This type typically needs ongoing, evidence-based management — cupping alone is unlikely to reverse it.

Stress, Postpartum & Telogen Effluvium

Sudden, diffuse shedding triggered by stress, illness, surgery, or childbirth is usually temporary and often resolves within months as the underlying trigger passes. Many clients try cupping during this period for general stress and tension relief alongside standard care.

Is Hijama Right for You? Safety & Who Should Avoid It

⚠️ Speak to a doctor first if any of the following apply to you:

  • Pregnancy
  • Bleeding disorders or severe anemia
  • Currently taking blood thinners
  • Active scalp infection, open wounds, or a flare-up of a scalp condition such as psoriasis

If none of these apply, hijama performed by a certified practitioner with sterile, disposable equipment is generally well tolerated.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is hijama effective for hair loss?

Many people feel their scalp is healthier and less tense after cupping, and it's believed to support local circulation — but there isn't strong clinical-trial evidence proving hair regrowth, so we recommend it as a complementary practice alongside proper diagnosis.

What is hijama for hair loss?

It's wet or dry cupping applied to the scalp and nape of the neck, based on the traditional Sunnah cupping technique.

How does cupping help the scalp?

The suction is believed to draw blood flow to the treated area, which some believe supports nutrient delivery to hair follicles.

Does hijama regrow hair permanently?

No treatment — hijama included — can promise permanent regrowth; results vary by cause and person, and genetic pattern baldness typically needs ongoing management.

How many sessions are needed for hair fall?

This varies by individual; many clients start with a course of several sessions spaced a few weeks apart, reviewed with the practitioner.

Is hijama safe for women?

Yes, when done by a trained practitioner with sterile equipment; we also offer female therapists for home visits.

How much does hijama for hair loss cost in Dhaka?

Pricing depends on session type (wet/dry) and location; contact us on WhatsApp or call for the current rate.

Can hijama cure baldness?

No — hijama is not a proven cure for genetic baldness; it may be used as a complementary practice while other causes are properly diagnosed and treated.

Where are the hijama points for hair loss?

Points are typically placed on the crown, back of the head, and nape of the neck, chosen by the practitioner based on your scalp condition.

Is hijama painful?

Most clients describe mild suction pressure and a brief pinch during wet cupping; dry/machine cupping has no incision at all.

What's the difference between wet and dry cupping for hair?

Wet cupping includes small superficial skin nicks after suction; dry cupping uses suction only, with no skin break.

Can men and women both get hijama for hair loss?

Yes, the service is offered to both, with a female therapist available on request.

Is hijama better than Minoxidil?

They work differently — Minoxidil is a clinically studied topical treatment; hijama is a traditional practice. Some clients use both after consulting a doctor.

Is hijama better than PRP for hair loss?

PRP is a clinical procedure with more research behind it for certain hair loss types; hijama is a lower-cost, non-invasive traditional alternative some choose alongside it.

Who should avoid hijama for hair loss?

Pregnant women, people with bleeding disorders, severe anemia, or those on blood thinners should consult a doctor first.

Can hijama help with postpartum hair loss?

Many clients try it during this phase, though postpartum shedding is usually temporary and often resolves on its own within months.

Can hijama help with stress-related hair fall?

Cupping is often used for general stress and tension relief, which some clients feel supports overall scalp wellness.

How long does a hijama session take?

Typically 30–60 minutes, depending on scalp condition and treatment plan.

Do you provide home service for hijama?

Yes, our certified practitioners visit your home anywhere in Dhaka, usually within about 2 hours of confirmed booking.

Is the equipment sterile?

Yes — sterile cups, disposable blades, gloves, and antiseptic materials are used at every session.

What should I do before a hijama session for hair loss?

Avoid heavy meals right before, stay hydrated, and share any medical conditions or medications with your practitioner.

What should I do after a hijama session?

Keep the scalp clean and dry for the rest of the day, avoid direct sun exposure on treated areas, and follow any aftercare notes from your practitioner.

Can hijama cause hair loss to get worse?

When performed correctly by a trained practitioner, this is not expected; poor hygiene or technique from unqualified providers is the real risk, which is why certified, sterile equipment matters.

Is hijama halal and Sunnah-based?

Yes, hijama (cupping) is a practice with roots in Sunnah tradition, which is part of why many clients in Dhaka choose it.

Does hijama help with dandruff or scalp itchiness affecting hair fall?

Some clients report scalp comfort improvements, but a persistent scalp condition should be assessed by a dermatologist for proper diagnosis first.

Can teenagers get hijama for hair loss?

This should be discussed with the practitioner and, ideally, a doctor, especially for younger clients with early hair thinning.
